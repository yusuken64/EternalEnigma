ASSESSMENT

The recurring problem is that players cannot reliably read the game or predict the
result of an action. That affects movement, equipment, status effects, targeting,
saving, and navigation. The party system and persistent adventure are the strongest
reasons reviewers give for wanting to continue. Improving access to those strengths
is a better next milestone than adding more content.

"More polish" can be made concrete: show item effects before use, expose status
mechanics, reduce HUD obstruction, distinguish scenery from gameplay objects, make
short movement inputs predictable, and protect progress between dungeons.

PRIORITIZED FINDINGS

DD71-01 — historical opening blocker: checked home prompts now work
Evidence: Big Greg reported an Enter prompt that did nothing in the web build.
The developer reproduced a Space/Enter mismatch and described a future-build fix.
https://itch.io/post/17826817
https://itch.io/post/17827787
Follow-up status: the public WebGL build reports version 0.0.337. From a fresh
slot, the book, chest, and front door displayed Space and responded correctly;
the home key was acquired and the door unlocked. The reported home-opening
mismatch was not reproduced. This does not certify every later/rebound prompt.
Action: extend that default-key home verification through bakery, trainer, sleep,
dungeon entry, targeting, alternate/rebound keys, and controller prompts.
Acceptance: every displayed default, rebound, alternate, and controller hint invokes
the action it describes. Browser focus and UI-confirm/world-interact are tested
separately. Treat failure as a release blocker.

DD71-02 — P1: inventory hides information needed to make decisions
Evidence: both videos show item confusion; SPEC requests full stats. Vagrant's
Oblivion Draught action panel around 14:09 has actions but no effect explanation.
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HxmN7nEjaGo&t=849s
https://itch.io/post/17856436
Source-confirmed cause:
  Assets/Scripts/Dungeon/Game/Menu/PartyMenu.cs:125
    Description scroll and parchment are enabled only for the Skills tab.
  PartyMenuContexts.cs:43
    Inventory entries nevertheless build item descriptions and comparison data.
  PartyMenuContexts.cs:318 and ActionDialog.cs:90
    Dungeon inventory opens the item action dialog, which sets name/actions but
    does not display the item's description.
Equipment's detailed picker can expose information that inventory does not, which
explains why players sometimes discover descriptions only later.
The Arcane Wand issue is principally terminology: its asset grants Strength +6;
StatPreview.Diff labels that same attribute "Attack". A resulting total of 13 is
not evidence of an extra hidden +6 calculation.
Action: show description, all modifiers, requirements, current/resulting totals,
consumption, and turn cost on selection and before confirmation, consistently
across inventory, equipment, and shop.
Acceptance: a new player can explain what Oblivion Draught and Explosion do, and
why the wand changes their total, without using/equipping them experimentally.

DD71-03 — P1: the HUD and scene obscure tactical information
Evidence: SPEC's objective and target screenshots directly show obstruction and
misalignment. Both videos show crowded HUD layouts; the shop footer at elb 14:51
extends below its parchment.
https://img.itch.zone/aW1nLzMyMDI4Njg5LmpwZw==/original/xC7Okh.jpg
https://img.itch.zone/aW1nLzMyMDI4NzMwLmpwZw==/original/oKVnbm.jpg
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3CZBMwkz9pY&t=891s
Source: OpeningGuidance.cs:33 anchors a broad objective panel in the upper playfield;
line 76 keeps its height between 66 and 124 reference pixels. The camera follows
with a fixed offset; no player zoom control was found in the inspected gameplay
camera path.
Action: make the persistent objective compact/collapsible, collapse an idle event
feed, offer zoom, fix target/shop text containment, and preserve adjacent-tile
visibility with four party cards. Shorten binding hints instead of listing every
alternate key in a footer.
Acceptance: inspect 1280x720 and the intended WebGL viewport at default browser
scale, plus larger resolutions. Long names, four heroes, status icons, targeting,
shops, and rebinding must fit without masking the selected actor or nearby threats.
Separate issue: SPEC's LOS image and elb 10:31–10:46 show confusing wall/passage
visibility. They do not prove incorrect logical FOV. Reproduce corners, doorways,
multi-tile enemies, health-bar occlusion, and fog/mesh boundaries before changing
the sight algorithm.

DD71-04 — P1: movement precision and diagonal discoverability
Evidence: movement complaints recur in Vagrant, SPEC, Feelmaker, and elb's video.
Vagrant believes diagonal attacks are unavailable; SPEC reports alternate-key
turn-in-place failure and requests dedicated diagonal bindings.
https://itch.io/post/17874727
https://itch.io/post/17851945
Current source already supports eight facings and HoldPosition. The default binding
is Shift; PlayerController uses a 0.1-second movement interval and a 0.12-second
diagonal-release grace period. This is not evidence that diagonal attack is absent.
The alternate-key defect and exact overshoot mechanism remain unverified.
Action: teach aim-in-place with a visible diagonal target; provide optional direct
diagonal bindings. Measure tap/hold behavior separately from animation and camera
scale before tuning.
Acceptance: test controlled tap durations around the repeat threshold, long holds,
release during animation, rapid direction changes, WASD/arrows/rebound keys, and
controller input at varied frame rates and animation settings. No unintended extra
action after release; holding position must rotate without walking.

DD71-05 — P1: leaving the overworld can discard a substantial session
Evidence: Vagrant attempts to stop at 26:42 onward, then reloads an earlier home
save. At 27:15 the slot shows 320 gold and Riley alone; the resumed world later has
the previously opened gate locked.
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HxmN7nEjaGo&t=1602s
Current source: SaveSystem.Capture does not write to disk. CampaignTravelService
captures after dungeon completion, while ReturnToMenu reloads the saved snapshot.
CampaignSaving.Commit performs the actual disk write. GlobalSettings.cs:170 warns
that unsaved progress will be lost.
Classification: save-policy/usability problem, not demonstrated corruption or
silent loss without a warning.
Action: choose a clear policy for saving safely outside dungeons: an overworld save,
milestone autosaves, or suspend/resume separate from the bed checkpoint.
Acceptance: recruit, finish a repeatable dungeon, collect rewards, open a gate,
leave/relaunch from the overworld; verify all intended progress persists. Also
verify failure recovery and existing checkpoint/death rules.

DD71-06 — P1: the opening delays and obscures the game's distinguishing features
Evidence: elb finishes the starter dungeon but stops without recruiting or reaching
the overworld. Both recordings include searching for required town destinations.
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3CZBMwkz9pY
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HxmN7nEjaGo&t=119s
Action: give the current required destination a clear landmark/marker; reduce
redundant procedural directions; let instructions remain readable until dismissed;
distinguish the safe practice encounter from subsequent real enemies. Preview
companions and the world journey early, and guide the first recruitment/exit.
Improve skill copy: identify the stat basis of a percentage, distinguish rank from
mana cost, describe the next rank, and explain resource recovery.
Acceptance: a first-time player can find the trainer and dungeon, understand the
practice transition, and reach the party/overworld feature without external help.
Use completion and confusion observations rather than an arbitrary speedrun target.

DD71-07 — P1: encounter density and readability undermine positioning
Evidence: shiro's spider screenshot shows severe overlap; Vagrant struggles with a
large populated room, then uses Explosion around 12:07. The feedback supports an
encounter-readability/pacing problem, not a proven impossibility of winning.
https://img.itch.zone/aW1nLzMyMTI5NTcwLnBuZw==/original/gsNvZ0.png
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HxmN7nEjaGo&t=727s
Developer advice about fighting spiders in hallways is useful design intent that
the game needs to communicate before overwhelming the player.
Action: audit early enemy budgets, spawn/reproduction limits, retreat routes, body
overlap, and the teaching of chokepoints. Make explosive effects explicit, including
self/allied damage where applicable. The current Explosion item description is
vague even though its effect asset specifies damage.
Acceptance: test representative seeds/classes with and without that consumable.
A player should perceive multiple viable tactical responses; the required response
must not depend on guessing an unidentified item. Tune obstacles by their effect
on routes, not merely by reducing every room's prop count.

DD71-08 — P1: status indicators do not explain the rules
Evidence: shiro cannot tell what Frail does; Vagrant also questions it.
https://itch.io/post/17879335
Runtime correction: portrait status buttons exist, but clicking Frailty shows
only its name and a turn count, not the Strength penalty or stack/removal rules.
The earlier claim here that Frailty persists until cleansed was incorrect.
Although its Tick method is empty, the production prefab has TurnsLeft=1 and
Character.GetStatusEffectSideEffects removes it at that value. A real potion use
ended with no active Frailty; direct application reduced Strength 11 to 10 before
turn cleanup removed it. Resolve this lifecycle/documented-intent inconsistency
before describing the effect as permanent.
Action: show effect, stack count, duration model, and removal method on inspection;
make the new-debuff notification and portrait affordance discoverable.
Acceptance: after receiving Frailty, the player can identify its current penalty,
whether it expires, and how to remove it. Verify all status types rather than
copying one generic duration string onto persistent effects.

DD71-09 — P1 design review: last-hit XP conflicts with autonomous companions
Evidence: shiro reports an underleveled recruit; the developer confirms deliberate
last-hit XP. Vagrant's companion later gains levels, so companions are not wholly
unable to earn experience.
Current source: MovementAction.cs:523 awards AddXPAction to the attacker; that action
updates the specified character's experience (line 590).
Action: prototype shared active-party XP or a catch-up rule and compare it with the
current system. If retaining last-hit progression, explicitly teach it and provide
reliable ways for a weak recruit to contribute.
Acceptance: a newly recruited hero makes visible progress during a normal dungeon
without the player needing to sabotage the stronger hero's automatic attacks.
Balance choice, not a confirmed missing XP award.

DD71-10 — P2: bag pressure arrives before players can evaluate loot
Evidence: SPEC requests storage; both videos reach a full bag. Inventory.cs:8 sets
the default capacity to 10. The house chest creates a storage expectation.
Action: fix item comparison first, then evaluate capacity, stacking, quick disposal,
and a shared town stash. A paid capacity upgrade alone may preserve the frustrating
first impression. Clearly identify what the home chest does until storage exists.
Acceptance: observe a normal first dungeon's pickup/discard decisions and time
spent reopening menus; verify storage persistence if a stash is introduced.

DD71-11 — P2: scenery communicates the wrong interaction
Evidence: shiro mistakes decorative chest/book shapes for loot; Vagrant repeatedly
tries to interact with large inert knights.
Current source: EarlyDungeonStatue.cs explicitly creates a harmless, destructible
Black Knight-shaped prop. These are not evidence of broken enemy AI.
Action: give statues a stone treatment/pedestal/inspection name and make decorative
books/chests visually distinct from collectible/interactable versions. Preserve
consistent highlighting and cursor/selection feedback.
Acceptance: a new player can distinguish enemy, statue, pickup, and decoration
before committing an action.

DD71-12 — P2 defect reproduction: Controls paging can select Audio
Follow-up: independently reproduced with mouse, keyboard Enter, and gamepad South.
Controls page 2 to 3 and page 2 to 1 both switched to Audio: six observed boundary
failures. Disabling the selected paging button and refreshing navigation transfers
focus to Audio; GlobalSettings PreviewOnFocus activates it.
Action: preserve usable focus within Controls at a page boundary; verify both
directions with actual selected-button input and after rebinding completion.
Acceptance: Previous/Next changes the binding page while keeping Controls selected;
focus always lands on a visible, usable control.

DD71-13 — P2: long traversal and repetitive layouts need pacing constraints
Evidence: Vagrant finds towns/overworld oversized; shiro's minimap shows repetitive
pillar detours; SPEC requests safe directional running.
Action: tune procedural generation using measurable destination spacing, useful
route length, branch density, and repeated-obstacle limits. Consider a safe run
command that stops on newly detected danger, interaction, junction, or obstruction.
Do not implement the request as blindly skipping actions into traps.
Acceptance: sample varied seeds and compare walking time to meaningful decisions.
The pillar screenshot demonstrates awkward routing, not an unreachable destination.

DD71-14 — P2 investigation: music fatigue
Evidence: SPEC reports repetitive dungeon music.
Status: subjective feedback; soundtrack/audio was not independently listened to.
Action: conduct a representative-length listening pass before deciding between
more variation, longer loops, quieter arrangements, and intermittent silence.

DD71-15 — P2: the submission page undersells the current game
The six gallery images show an earlier HUD/town presentation than the November
videos, including old equipment naming and a large level-up overlay. They are
marketing evidence, not proof those old UI defects remain in the current build.
The cover animation emphasizes a character and enemies rather than the party
adventure. The short genre description does not explain the game's distinguishing
features.
Action: refresh gallery/cover with current party combat, readable UI, town
preparation, and overworld progression. Describe the controllable hero, autonomous
companions, and continuing adventure accurately.
