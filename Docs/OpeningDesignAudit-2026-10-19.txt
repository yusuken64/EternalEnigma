Opening 20 minutes: design and presentation audit
2026-10-19 | Working tree d858aca9

The strongest opportunities are a more intimate starting home, disciplined UI
spacing, clearer combat composition, and an opening that lets the player practise
what they learn. The game has useful ingredients: expressive heroes, furnished
interiors, a preparation-day structure, explicit saving, and a dungeon-clear
reward that opens the town gate. Their presentation needs a stronger hierarchy.

The user's specific direction is incorporated here:
- Make the starting house smaller.
- Replace the first house's unappealing carpet colour.
- Put the dungeon floor indicator beside the minimap.
- Keep text comfortably inside its containing box.

Quality criteria
----------------
For this audit, the Nintendo quality reference means understandable actions,
careful composition from the gameplay camera, feedback that explains events,
learning through small successes, and moments of character and surprise.
These are review criteria, not a claim of equivalence to a Nintendo production.
Nintendo's Super Mario Bros. Wonder developers describe tuning character poses
to communicate state and direction from the actual gameplay view, and matching
sound timing to the visible action. That is a useful standard for this game:
https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2023/October/Ask-the-Developer-Vol-11-Super-Mario-Bros-Wonder-Chapter-2-2460633.html

1. First priority: give the starting home its own compact composition
------------------------------------------------------------------
Observed: the seed-42 home capture has broad areas of stone flooring, furniture
around the perimeter, and a long central teal carpet. Much of the room lies
beyond the captured view. The supplied criticism that the house feels too big
is consistent with this composition.

There is a structural reason: furnished building plots are generated to fit the
largest service-room recipe. FurnishedRandom chooses bodies 9 or 11 cells wide
and 9-12 cells deep; its fallback is 9x9. These are generator ranges, not a
measurement of the exact captured home. The home inherits that generous plot
system even though residential furnishing can use a smaller room.

Recommended direction:
- Author a compact opening-home arrangement. Prototype roughly 5x5 to 5x7 usable
  floor cells, then fit it to the actual gameplay camera and movement clearance.
- Compose the hero, functional bed and exit as the main three readable elements.
  Cluster furniture into a sleeping corner and a small living corner.
- Keep a short, obvious route from the wake-up position to the exit. Check that
  the bed, doorway and following party remain usable after reducing the room.
- Let the opening camera show enough of the doorway to invite the first move.
  Judge the result at the player's camera scale, with the HUD present.

Success: on the opening view, a new player can find their character, recognise
the bed and identify the exit without searching around a large empty room.

Evidence: Temp/OpeningPreparation/home-before-errands.png;
Core/EternalEnigma.Core/EternalEnigma.Core/Generation/BuildingShapes.cs:18;
Core/EternalEnigma.Core/EternalEnigma.Core/Generation/TownInteriorGenerator.cs:22.

2. First priority: redesign the room palette and carpet together
--------------------------------------------------------------
Observed: the carpet is a large, nearly uniform teal area with a warm border;
the bed blanket repeats the teal. Its flat surface and large area make it read
more like a coloured floor panel than a small domestic textile. The grey stone
floor also gives the home a similar material character to the dungeon.

Recommended direction: a smaller woven rug in muted terracotta, warm timber or
quieter warm-grey flooring, cream trim, and restrained textile accents. A useful
initial palette to prototype is rug #956451, trim #CEB88F and wood #78533E. These
are proposed colours, not sampled values or approved final art.

Give the rug a modest border and subtle material variation. Restrict its area
to a living or bedside zone. Keep the hero and interactive bed distinct from
the surfaces behind them. Review this as a complete room palette under game
lighting; changing the carpet's hue alone will leave the scale problem intact.

Evidence: home-before-errands.png; the carpet recipe at
Core/EternalEnigma.Core/EternalEnigma.Core/Generation/TownInteriorGenerator.cs:81
fills a three-cell-wide central strip over most of the room's depth.

3. First priority: establish padding and reading order across the UI
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Observed: in the fresh 960x600 bakery capture, item names begin within a few
pixels of the button's left boundary and prices nearly touch the right boundary.
Footer instructions also sit close to the parchment edge. The trainer is more
spacious, but its first ability explanation appears after rank, tier, level,
cost, availability and prerequisite text.

Recommended layout standards, expressed at a 960x600 reference resolution:
- Start with 24-32 px of panel padding inside the decorative border.
- Start with 16-24 px of horizontal padding and 8-12 px of vertical padding in
  item rows and buttons. Adjust for the actual font metrics and ornament shape.
- Use a padded inner content rectangle. Keep labels and prices in separate
  aligned columns, with a protected gap between them.
- Give titles, descriptions, secondary metadata and control hints distinct
  visual weights. Keep decorative lettering for short display text.
- Reflow or scroll long content before shrinking it below comfortable reading
  size. The selected item's full name and effect should remain accessible.
- Maintain visible focus using an outline or marker as well as colour.

For the first Double Strike preview, lead with its effect, then show its cost
and the Learn action. Put rank and unlock rules beneath that primary decision
information. Show the actual Mana cost and targeting information from skill data.

Success: no visible glyph touches the inner frame; long item names and prices
fit together; the player can explain an ability before purchasing it. Check the
same layout at 960x600, 1280x720, 1920x1080 and an ultrawide aspect ratio.

Evidence: Temp/OpeningPreparation/free-bread.png and first-lesson.png;
Assets/Scripts/Town/Menu/BallistaDialog.cs:180.

4. First priority: give the HUD clear groups and protect combat readability
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Observed: the live dungeon shows a detached "2F" beneath the top menu tabs. The
captured early fight combines large overlapping enemies, vivid pink enemy base
discs and a bright impact burst over the controlled hero. That captured instant
is difficult to parse. It does not establish how long the overlap persists.

Recommended direction:
- Place "Floor 2 / 5" immediately beside or above the minimap, aligned to its
  edge with the same padding system. Keep this location group together as map
  modes and screen sizes change.
- Reserve stable HUD regions for party condition, navigation/progress, resource
  totals and menu access. Keep the central combat area as clear as possible.
- Maintain a visible hero silhouette, facing and occupied tile through attacks.
  Reduce the opaque centre and excessive reach of effects that cover the hero.
- Use restrained hostile markers, and a stronger outline/shape for the current
  target. Distinguish hostile, selected and controlled states beyond colour alone.
- Make action -> impact -> HP/status change easy to connect. Let the event log
  supplement that visual explanation.
- Consider revealing the Full Control feature when a companion joins, with a
  short explanation. Its persistent label adds a concept before a solo player
  has an immediate reason to use it.

Success: a player can locate the hero, the selected target, current floor and
next available action at a glance during a normal-speed encounter.

Evidence: live dungeon inspection and
Logs/Playthroughs/20261020-004944-dd0b101f/run-0001-seed-42-50b2eb06/final.png;
Assets/Scripts/Dungeon/Game/UI/DungeonHud.cs:100.

5. High priority: make preparation teach actions through small successes
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Observed: the current opening requires free bread, an active-skill purchase,
then sleep. The first lesson protects the player's points from being spent on
passives. Home explains that defeat returns to the last save, and morning
confirms that preparations were saved. These are useful improvements.

The remaining gap is that learning a skill satisfies the opening requirement
without demonstrating its use. The first training view also exposes blocked and
completed rows alongside the one currently available primary active skill. The
free-bread screen includes expensive products before the first transaction is
understood.

Recommended sequence:
- Use the bedroom to teach movement and one contextual interaction.
- Let the baker offer a clear "Take your free bread" action, explain what food
  restores, and acknowledge the receipt. Provide the full shop as a secondary
  option during this first visit.
- Let the trainer recommend a suitable first active skill and immediately offer
  a brief practice encounter that demonstrates its effect and target rules.
  Support non-damaging skills with an appropriate practice target or situation.
- Present one readable first opponent before mixed groups; then combine a known
  enemy with one new behaviour. Introduce recovery before a likely first defeat.
- Make a small interaction memorable: the baker handing over the loaf, a dummy
  visibly reacting, or the morning light and gate changing after sleep. Tie each
  detail to something the player just accomplished.

Success: before the first dangerous group, the player has successfully used an
ability, understands its resource, and knows how to use a recovery item.

Evidence: first-lesson.png, free-bread.png, ready-for-morning.png;
Core/EternalEnigma.Core/EternalEnigma.Core/Progression/CampaignContext.cs:91;
Assets/Scripts/Town/TrainerOffers.cs:27; Assets/Scripts/Town/HomeBed.cs:112.

6. High priority: keep the next goal visible and shorten required errands
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Observed: opening guidance is posted into the event feed. Outside dungeon turns,
that feed starts fading after nine seconds and disappears after eleven. It is
not a persistent current-objective display. The controlled morning/gate capture
still contains earlier "ready for tomorrow" history beneath the open gate.

Town services are distributed procedurally. The seed-42 automated route recorded
188 town movement actions before entering the dungeon. This route uses full map
knowledge, does additional preparation, and refreshes the party; it is not a
measurement of a novice player's route, walking time, or minimum required steps.
It does identify the distance between required destinations as worth examining.

Recommended direction:
- Keep one short current objective visible: collect bread -> learn/practise a
  skill -> sleep -> enter the dungeon -> clear it -> return and save.
- Update that objective directly when the state changes. Keep historical event
  messages visually distinct from the next action.
- Give the opening home, bakery and trainer a readable local arrangement, clear
  door signs and landmarks. Constrain their separation across starting seeds.
- Offer contextual movement/interact prompts until the player demonstrates them.
  Keep prompts brief and matched to the active input device.

Success: the player can answer "Where am I going, and why?" after returning from
a menu, after the message feed fades, and after waking the next morning.

Evidence: Assets/Scripts/MainMenu/GameMessages.cs:198;
Assets/Scripts/Town/Town.cs:375; Temp/OpeningPreparation/entrance-open.png;
the fresh run's actions.jsonl; Generation/TownLayout.cs.

7. Medium priority: make hero selection communicate playstyle
-----------------------------------------------------------
Observed: the picker displays 24 heroes at once. Its preview provides a name,
class pairing and primary role, but no signature action or concrete example of
how the choice changes play.

Recommended direction: give each preview one plain-language playstyle sentence
and one signature-action example. Offer a small recommended starting selection
while preserving access to the full roster. Give confirmation a clear label,
such as "Begin as Rowan", that is visually separate from browsing the roster.
Keep mouse preview and activation behaviour predictable.

Success: a new player can describe how three options differ without needing to
interpret the class system first.

Evidence: live hero-picker inspection;
Assets/Scripts/MainMenu/ProtagonistHeroPicker.cs:188.

8. Medium priority: strengthen the world's material and colour hierarchy
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Observed: the town mixes strongly mottled grass, faceted trees, large tiled
facades, small heroes and parchment UI. The early dungeon uses conspicuous base
colours and effects. These elements compete for attention at gameplay scale.

Recommended direction: soften high-frequency ground detail, use broader quiet
material shapes, and reserve stronger contrast for the hero, useful doors,
interactables and threats. Give the home a warmer domestic material identity.
Keep world labels supported by a consistent outline or quiet backing. Review
lighting, texture scale, props and UI together in actual gameplay screenshots.

Success: the scene remains legible when viewed small or in greyscale; important
interactive objects retain clear silhouettes and locations without relying only
on saturation. Check moving footage before judging animation quality.

9. Medium priority: make the first clear produce an unmistakable payoff
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The reward code already tells the player that the town gate is open and the
overworld is available. Preserve that clarity. Add a brief visible world change
or reveal, a clear next objective, and a reminder to bank the new progress at
home. The first clear currently captures progress in memory; it does not itself
commit a durable save.

The opening should finish with a feeling of competence: the player used their
class, overcame an understandable challenge, changed the world, and knows their
progress is safe. A short gate reveal and a glimpse of the next destination can
provide that sense of arrival.

Evidence: Assets/Scripts/Overworld/CampaignTravelService.cs, FinishDungeon and
ShowKeyRewards; Docs/CampaignSaves.md. This recommendation is source-based; the
fresh probe was stopped on floor 2 before a clear.

Proposed opening rhythm to validate with new players
--------------------------------------------------
0-2 minutes: choose a hero, wake in a readable home, move and interact.
2-5 minutes: receive food, practise a signature ability, sleep/save, see morning.
5-10 minutes: read a simple fight, use the ability, receive loot, recover once.
10-15 minutes: combine familiar mechanics with one new encounter idea.
15-20 minutes: clear the first dungeon, see the gate open, save, glimpse the world.
These are proposed pacing targets, not measured playthrough times.

Recommended order of work
-------------------------
First pass: compact starting home and palette; shared UI padding; floor/minimap
grouping. These directly address the user's observations and improve many frames.
Second pass: persistent objective, simple first transactions and real skill
practice, followed by staged first combat.
Third pass: wider material/lighting consistency, restrained combat feedback and
the first-clear reveal. Validate timing and player comprehension throughout.

Implementation follow-up, 2026-10-19
----------------------------------
New campaigns now use a compact 5x5 usable home with one functional bed,
perimeter furnishings, warm wood flooring, a small muted terracotta rug with
cream edging, and sage linen. Home, bakery and trainer have fixed nearby
opening positions. Existing campaign layouts retain their saved version.

Authored dialogs, inventory/action rows and shop/trainer panels have consistent
text insets. Floor progress is grouped directly above the minimap and remains
visible when only the map texture is hidden. The solo-party Full Control toggle
is hidden; its purpose is introduced when companions join.

Hero selection starts with three distinct recommendations, retains all 24 heroes,
explains playstyle and an actual class ability, and uses an explicit Begin action.
The bakery offers free bread directly, then a short receipt and next step. Its
full stock remains available through Browse. The first trainer highlights one
usable active lesson, with effect, cost and targeting explained before purchase.

Persistent objectives are separate from fading events. A harmless optional
training target uses real ability costs and targeting; successful practice leads
to one ordinary opponent. Recovery guidance responds to actual meters/items.
The first dungeon clear reveals the opened gate and reminds the player to save.
These lessons apply to new campaigns and keep the stairs available throughout.

Ordinary melee impacts are smaller and shorter. Hostile ground markers use a
thin interrupted outline; allied markers remain continuous. Town ground detail
and opening-street decoration are quieter.

The save-slot biome art has been regenerated from editable Unity diorama scenes,
with a cottage and a distinct biome setting in each of the eight landscapes.
Shorelines are continuous, the shore cottage has a supported plank dock, and
the volcanic picture includes a visible lava stream. Card artwork uses a centred
crop to retain its proportions; slot labels have inset margins and a shadow.
All eight final images and their card presentation were visually reviewed.

NPC dialogue audit, 2026-10-19
-----------------------------
Scope: the opening town and first return, plus the recurring dialogue mechanisms
that shape later towns. This section is based on production source, authored copy
and the service-menu captures. It is not a timed conversation playthrough.

Assessment by the requested criteria:
- Substance: greetings supply pleasant mood, but rarely a concrete fact about
  the speaker, a personal concern, or a reason the protagonist matters to them.
- Relevance: all ambient residents append the same global progression hint,
  regardless of their occupation or whether its destination has been discovered.
- Balance: a short character line can be followed by a long mechanical explanation.
  Repeated conversations repeat this material, alongside the persistent objective.
- Usefulness: actionable direction is valuable. It loses trust when biographies
  contradict the actual class, or a next-step instruction ignores preparation state.
- Narrative weight: the gate opens after victory, but people do not acknowledge
  the accomplishment; their shared hint moves directly to the next obstacle.
- Appropriateness: the warm animal-town tone fits the presentation. Some later
  generated situations introduce human stakes that generic access feedback does
  not adequately resolve.

Priority findings and source evidence:
1. Ambient NPCs share a walkthrough voice. TownNpc.cs:13-16 concatenates name,
   static Greeting and CampaignGuidance.TownHint. CampaignGuidance.cs:48-82 chooses
   an unresolved required route and can enumerate solutions and source locations.
   Give each resident a particular area of knowledge; detailed directions should
   be an optional follow-up. Use short repeat lines and milestone variants.

2. Named vendors mostly lose their identity in play. TownPlayer.cs:217-225 triggers
   an animation and opens the service menu. Only TownNpc.Greet reads Greeting;
   TownInteriorRendering spawns only Cat/Dog as ambient conversational NPCs.
   Mallow/Pip/Bram/Poppy/Vesper's authored greeting lines therefore do not reach
   the player through ordinary service interactions. Show a keeper's name and
   one brief personal line within the existing service menu.

3. The opening has practical tasks without a resident-owned reason to care.
   CampaignContext.OpeningPreparationHint explains bread, training and sleep;
   LockSkinCatalog.TownGateKeyText explains the key at the dungeon bottom.
   A brief local concern, why the player can help, and a reason to return would
   give those actions emotional purpose. Keep the stakes small and concrete.

4. The first victory has no social response. CampaignTravelService provides the
   gate and save payoff, but TownNpcDefinition stores only one greeting. Give one
   or two familiar residents a first-return acknowledgment before the next lead.

5. Companion Talk is a biography/party-management screen. AllyRecruitDialog.cs:62
   uses the same third-person Description; its Talk branch at :90 shows removal
   controls. Rowan's Ally_MC03 biography at :2917 calls a Warrior/Healer a rogue;
   Avery's Ally_MC01 biography at :3423 calls a Warrior/Occultist a skilled mage.
   Reconcile fiction with actual kits, state why a companion wants to join, and
   distinguish personal conversation from roster administration.

6. Serious generated situations need human resolution. LockSkinCatalog.cs:233
   describes a child with fever; :239 describes a quarrel blocking a road.
   OverworldScene.cs:449-454 and CampaignTravelService.cs:228-235 resolve access
   with generic 'The way is now open!' feedback. Give these families a short
   outcome for the people involved, or reduce the stakes to match the interaction.

7. Identity repetition reduces credibility. The same named Cat/Dog residents are
   reused in generated towns, and Rowan the Shepherd shares the playable Rowan's
   name. Reserve unique names for persistent people, or establish a recurring
   traveler deliberately.

One factual instruction defect found in this audit was corrected within the
opening-flow implementation: the first trainer success message now derives its
next step from OpeningObjective, so visiting the trainer before the bakery no
longer incorrectly directs the player straight to bed. The broader dialogue
rewrite remains a recommendation.

Suggested writing budget: one specific personal sentence, one relevant instruction
when needed, and a short reaction on return. Add first-meeting, preparation/morning,
first-clear and repeat variants before adding more lore. Preserve the clear cost,
effect and next-action information already present in the revised service menus.

First-home capability follow-up, 2026-10-19
-----------------------------------------
New campaigns now begin with a book, chest and locked front door in the first
home. The book explains that the chest holds the Home Key, the first acquired
capability, and that it unlocks the door. The chest grants that permanent
capability once; a facing interaction at the door uses it. Reading is optional
if the player discovers the chest first. Contextual prompts use the current
input device, and the book, chest and door visuals reflect their saved state.

HomeLessonVersion gates this sequence to new campaigns. Older saves bypass it.
Home Key is excluded from procedural campaign capability pools, preserving the
existing campaign generator's choices. Book, key and door state are captured by
normal saving. Locked-home movement and travel are blocked, including diagonal
escape. Bed instructions now follow the active home objective. The existing
Capabilities menu is also available in campaign towns, with a Home Key detail
that explains the door interaction and updates after unlocking.

The functional bed is restricted to the single first-home anchor. Decorative
beds in other interiors are benches; cached decorative Bed placements also
render as benches. Inns retain their service interaction.

The first staged production-scene review reached bookRead=True, key=True and
doorUnlocked=True with exactly one HomeBed. Core built with zero errors, and
Unity scripts compiled with zero errors and eleven existing warnings. These
captures are layout and integration previews, not a timed twenty-minute human
playthrough. No test suite was run.

Following the user's visual correction, MessageDialog's full-screen outer
parchment is removed. A transparent input shield remains behind the single
content panel. The authoring pass now preserves this arrangement, and generic
styling excludes modal backdrops, dimmers and input shields so they do not
become decorative parchment again.

Final layout review: the repeated production-scene capture completed through
floor two at 960x600 and 1280x720, including the locked bed instructions, book,
key reward, opened chest, capability list/detail, and unlocked front door.
The single parchment message panel leaves the room visible around it. The key
detail uses 32-reference-pixel body text, inset margins and plain source wording.
One final book-copy punctuation correction follows the screenshot review because
the existing presentation formatter strips colons. No automated tests were run.
